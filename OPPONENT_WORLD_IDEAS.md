# Opponent diversity and a living boxing world

Follow-up ideas requested while implementing observational strategy and the jump-rope artwork.
Implemented locally: an optional BOXING WORLD view of current belt holders, division contenders, authoritative P4P top 15, fighter profiles, and news tied to simulated bouts, title changes, retirements and occasional weight moves. NPCs have individual pace and secondary tendencies. The remaining ideas below are a design backlog; no specific real boxer is copied.

## Distinct people within each style

Build fictional opponents by combining traits inspired by researched real boxing careers.
Before using a specific real fighter as a reference, verify their relevant characteristics
against primary material such as bout footage, interviews and official career records.

Separate stance, reach, preferred range, pace, punch selection, defensive habits, adaptability,
endurance curve and career stage. A style label alone must not determine every behaviour.
Nationalities and flags identify characters; movement and tactical strengths come from their
individual attributes, training backgrounds and experience.

Candidate templates:

- Tall jab-focused prospect who needs room and resets after exchanges.
- Short pressure fighter who invests in body attacks over several rounds.
- Veteran counterpuncher with low volume and carefully timed responses.
- Explosive combination puncher whose activity drops late.
- Durable, economical contender who changes tempo in the second half.
- Mobile southpaw who repeatedly changes the angle after attacking.
- Former champion rebuilding after a loss, with a career history that supports the description.

Introduce traits through observations and actual behaviour. Avoid displaying an automatic
counter-strategy, a hidden combat formula or an assured winning choice.

## FM-like world activity

The player's calendar should sit inside a shared simulated world calendar.

1. Maintain identifiable champions and ranked contenders in each division.
2. Give P4P contenders a profile, recent results and a readable reason for their standing.
3. Schedule NPC title defences, eliminators and contender bouts with preparation and recovery.
4. Let actual NPC results change champions, rankings, rematches, activity and career trajectories.
5. Add motivated division changes, layoffs, returns and retirement as careers develop.
6. Publish coverage grounded in those events: previews, results, upsets, title changes and P4P movement.
7. Connect important changes to the player's opportunities: an eliminator, newly available title,
   a rival's rise, a returning former champion or a champion changing divisions.

The same results must drive records, titles, rankings, biographies and news. No fabricated news
about fights that the simulation did not resolve. Division changes must update title eligibility
and rankings consistently; history remains attached to stable fighter IDs.

## Show the world without overwhelming the player

- Keep the viewpoint of one boxer. The simulation runs the wider world in the background;
  the player does not manage scouting departments, other fighters, matchmaking organisations
  or a manager's career. World information should explain the boxer's own sporting context.
- Show the current division champion and a small set of relevant contenders by default.
- Add short opponent introductions with stakes and a link to the profile.
- Surface only consequential world news on the main career screen.
- Put full rankings, P4P profiles, schedules, title lineage and old articles in an optional world view.
- Notify on actual meaningful changes rather than generating a headline for every weekly tick.

Suggested order: individual opponent profiles and visible champion identities, a reliable world
event ledger, ranking and title updates, event-based world journalism, then broader career arcs.
